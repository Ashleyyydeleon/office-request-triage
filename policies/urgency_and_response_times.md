# Urgency levels and response times

The workplace team sorts every request into one of three urgency levels. The level
depends on the impact on people, not on how the message is worded. Capital letters,
exclamation marks or the word "urgent" do not make a request high on their own.

## High: respond within 15 minutes
Someone is in danger, or people are blocked from working or entering right now.
Examples: fire, smoke or burning smells, gas smells, flooding or active water leaks,
injuries and medical emergencies, people trapped (elevators, locked rooms), security
threats or intruders, building-wide outages, guests or meetings that are stuck right now.
For danger to people, call 911 and building security first, then file the request.

## Medium: respond within 1 business day
Something is broken or missing and affects someone's work, but nobody is at risk and
work can continue for now. Examples: equipment not working at a reserved desk, a
missing booking for an upcoming meeting, a lost personal item, a clogged sink, a
broken lock or reader with a workaround, a temperature problem on one floor.

## Low: respond within 3 business days
Questions, restocks, small inconveniences and requests for later. Examples: how-to
questions, supply restocks, snack requests, future seating or room changes, schedule
and policy questions.

When unsure between medium and low, use low. When unsure whether something could
put people at risk, treat it as high.
